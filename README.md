## 3. Verify the lawyer’s license and professional standing

Before hiring any attorney, check whether they are licensed and eligible to practice in California. The State Bar of California provides an attorney search tool that allows the public to check license status and disciplinary or administrative history.

Before signing a contract, verify:

The attorney is licensed in California
The attorney is active and eligible to practice
The attorney’s profile matches the law firm’s claims
There are no concerning disciplinary issues
The firm provides clear attorney information

Good reviews are helpful, but licensing verification is still one of the most important steps when choosing legal representation.

## 5. Ask about fees and payment structure

Most personal injury lawyers work on a contingency fee basis, meaning the lawyer receives a percentage of the recovery instead of charging hourly fees upfront. Forbes Advisor explains that most personal injury lawyers charge on contingency, and some may also charge administrative or case-related costs.

Before hiring a lawyer, ask:

Is the consultation free?
Do I pay anything upfront?
What percentage does the lawyer take?
Are case costs separate from attorney fees?
Are costs deducted before or after attorney fees?
What happens if we do not win?
Will I receive a written fee agreement?

## 7. Ask the right consultation questions

The consultation should help you decide whether the lawyer is a good fit. Do not only ask, “How much is my case worth?” A better consultation should reveal the lawyer’s experience, process, communication style, and fee terms.

Ask:

Have you handled cases like mine before?
What challenges do you see in my case?
Who will manage my case day to day?
How often will I receive updates?
What evidence should I preserve?
Should I speak with the insurance company?
Are you prepared to file a lawsuit if settlement fails?
What damages may apply to my claim?
What fees and costs should I expect?

A good lawyer should give realistic answers. Be careful with anyone who promises a guaranteed settlement amount before reviewing the facts.

## 8. Watch for red flags

Not every personal injury lawyer is the right choice. Some firms may rely heavily on advertising but provide poor communication or unclear expectations.

Avoid lawyers who:

Guarantee a specific settlement
Pressure you to sign immediately
Avoid explaining fees
Do not provide a written fee agreement
Have poor communication during intake
Do not focus on personal injury
Cannot explain who will handle your case
Discourage you from checking reviews or license status

A trustworthy lawyer should make you feel informed, not pressured.

## Frequently Asked Questions
1. How soon should I contact a personal injury lawyer after an accident?

It is best to contact a lawyer as soon as possible, especially if you were injured, fault is disputed, evidence may disappear, or an insurance adjuster is asking for a statement. California Courts notes that injured people should keep evidence related to the injury.

2. How long do I have to file a personal injury claim in California?

Many California personal injury cases generally have a 2-year deadline from the injury, though exceptions may apply depending on the facts.

3. Do personal injury lawyers charge upfront fees?

Many personal injury lawyers work on contingency, meaning they take a percentage of the recovery instead of charging hourly fees upfront.

4. Should I check a lawyer’s license before hiring?

Yes. The State Bar of California allows the public to check an attorney’s license status and disciplinary or administrative history.
